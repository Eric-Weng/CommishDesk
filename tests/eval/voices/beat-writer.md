Trench Warfare — 2025 Draft Recap

## The Lead

All 72 picks are in, six rounds deep, and the shape of this draft was set before
the first round was over. Five of the first eleven picks were running backs, and
two managers took a quarterback in round 1 — in a 2QB, Half PPR, dynasty league,
that is a room that decided early it was not going to get caught short at the
position that starts twice.

Round 1 went five running backs, five wide receivers, two quarterbacks. No one
reached for a tight end, no one reached for a kicker, and the two quarterbacks
who did go — one at 1.05, one at 1.10 — went to managers who clearly were not
going to spend round 3 sweating the QB board.

Pull-Guard Pumas made twelve picks, seven of them in round four, and finished
with the most selections of anyone in the league. On the other end, Man-Coverage
Meerkats made three. That is the spread this recap keeps coming back to: one
roster treated the middle rounds like a clearance sale, another treated every
pick like it cost money.

Gridiron Gophers drafted six running backs. Nobody else in the league drafted
more than four at any position. If you want the one-sentence version of this
draft, it is that the Gophers looked at a rookie class and decided the answer was
volume at running back, and the Pumas looked at the same class and decided the
answer was volume, full stop.

## The Board — Round 1

Pull-Guard Pumas opened it with Ashton Jeanty at 1.01. The consensus board had
Jeanty at 1.04, so this was a reach of three slots — the biggest reach in the
entire draft, and it came on the very first pick. Whether that ages into a bargain
or a cautionary tale, the Pumas are on record: they wanted him and they were not
going to risk the wait.

Field-Goal Foxes went back to back at 1.02 and 1.03 with Omarion Hampton and
Travis Hunter. Hampton at 1.02 was a slot ahead of his 1.01 consensus, Hunter at
1.03 a slot ahead of his 1.02 — two picks, two small values, a clean start.

Iron-Range Ibex took Tetairoa McMillan at 1.04, three slots ahead of his 1.07
consensus. That is the second-biggest reach on the board, and it is the kind of
reach that looks fine if the player is a stud and looks rough if he is the fourth
receiver off a board that had him seventh.

Play-Action Pandas took Cameron Ward at 1.05, the first quarterback off the
board. He had no consensus slot at all, so there is nothing to measure it
against — the Pandas were pricing a rookie passer in a 2QB league on their own
read, and in this format that is a defensible place to spend a first-rounder.

Blitz Alpacas took TreVeyon Henderson at 1.06, and the board loved it: a
three-slot value against a 1.03 consensus. Then Gridiron Gophers went running
back and receiver on 1.07 and 1.08 — Quinshon Judkins a slot of value at 1.07,
Emeka Egbuka three slots of reach at 1.08 against an 1.11 consensus.

End-Around Egrets took Matthew Golden at 1.09, right on the number. Coverage
Capybaras took Jaxson Dart at 1.10, the second quarterback of the round and a
five-slot value against a 1.05 consensus — the best pure value in round 1.
Blitz Alpacas came back at 1.11 for RJ Harvey, a slot of value. And Fair-Catch
Ferrets closed the round at 1.12 with Luther Burden, a four-slot reach against a
1.08 consensus.

## Superlatives

Best value: Lan Larison to Blitz Alpacas at 6.11. The board had Larison at 5.06.
To land him seventeen slots later, on the second-to-last pick any manager would
make, is the single largest gap between a pick and its consensus in this draft.
If one late pick decides a dynasty roster's floor this year, the Alpacas already
have their candidate.

Best value, runner-up: Gunnar Helm to Play-Action Pandas at 6.05, twelve slots
past his 5.05 consensus. A tight end that late, in a league where ten of them
came off the board, is exactly the kind of pick you forget you made until it
starts every week.

Biggest reach: Ashton Jeanty to Pull-Guard Pumas at 1.01, three slots ahead of
his 1.04 consensus. When your reach is the first overall pick, you are not
hedging — you are telling the league you would rather be wrong on your terms than
right on someone else's.

Biggest reach, runner-up: Tetairoa McMillan to Iron-Range Ibex at 1.04, also
three slots ahead of consensus. Two of the first four picks were reaches on
receivers. Round 1 was not a patient round.

Boldest swing: Blitz Alpacas. They spent 2.11 on Jalen Royals, a slot ahead of
his 2.12 consensus, and then landed Lan Larison at 6.11, seventeen slots of
value. The widest spread between a value and a reach on any one roster belongs to
the Alpacas, and it is not close.

## Team Grades

Grades weigh each pick against the consensus board, the premium-position capital
a roster spent, and how the roster fits a 2QB, dynasty format.

Pull-Guard Pumas: A+. Twelve picks, six of them a straight run from 42 to 47, and
the draft's top grade. The reach on Jeanty at 1.01 is the headline, but the pick
that earns the grade is Tai Felton at 54 — ten slots of value in a round where
the Pumas were just piling up bodies. That is volume plus one real hit, and a
good draft.

Blitz Alpacas: A+. Six picks, and the ones that carry the grade are TreVeyon
Henderson at 1.06 and Lan Larison at 6.11. The Larison pick alone — seventeen
slots of value — is the best single swing in the league. The Alpacas left the
room with the class's biggest bargain.

Coverage Capybaras: A. Only four picks, one at each of QB, RB, WR and TE, and
they still found value: Tez Johnson at 58 went ten slots past his consensus. Jaxson
Dart at 1.10 was a five-slot value at quarterback. A small, sharp draft.

End-Around Egrets: A. Five picks, and Isaiah Bond at 69 is the one that matters —
eleven slots of value on the second-to-last-round board. Matthew Golden at 1.09
was right on the number, a fair price for a receiver that early.

Fair-Catch Ferrets: A. Five picks, three receivers, and Nick Nash at 61 landed
ten slots ahead of his consensus. The reach on Luther Burden at 1.12 — four slots
— is the cost, and in a dynasty league a first-round receiver you like is a
reasonable thing to overpay for.

Gridiron Gophers: A. Eleven picks and six running backs, more at one position
than anyone else in the league. Emeka Egbuka at 1.08 was a three-slot reach, but
Elic Ayomanor at 38 was a six-slot value, and the sheer depth of the running back
room is its own kind of insurance in a dynasty format.

Field-Goal Foxes: A-. Six picks, four receivers, and the back-to-back start of
Omarion Hampton and Travis Hunter at 1.02 and 1.03 set a clean tone. Arian Smith
at 63 was a seven-slot value. The Foxes did not take a running back until late,
which is the one thing to flag.

Play-Action Pandas: A-. Six picks, two quarterbacks — including Cameron Ward at
1.05 — which in a 2QB league is a plan, not an accident. Gunnar Helm at 6.05,
twelve slots of value at tight end, is the pick that pushes the grade up.

Goal-Line Gazelles: B+. Five picks, three of them running backs. Antwane Wells at
72 — the very last pick of the draft — went twelve slots ahead of his consensus,
which is a nice way to end a quiet draft. Ollie Gordon at 32 was a one-slot
reach, barely worth mentioning.

Iron-Range Ibex: B+. Five picks, and the McMillan reach at 1.04 is the one the
league will remember. The counter is Kyle McCord at 64 — nine slots of value at
quarterback, which in this format is not a throwaway.

Long-Snap Lynx: B+. Four picks, two receivers and two tight ends, and no running
back at all — the only roster in the league to leave the draft without one.
Jalin Conyers at 67 was a ten-slot value, so the tight end plan at least paid.

Man-Coverage Meerkats: B+. Three picks, fewest in the league, and two of them
running backs. Donovan Edwards at 68 went nine slots ahead of consensus. When you
only make three picks, you cannot afford a miss, and the Meerkats did not take an
obvious one — Cam Skattebo at 20 was a three-slot reach at most.

## Positional Read

Quarterback: ten drafted, six of them through the end of round 3, the first at
1.05. It ran early and it ran hard, which is what a 2QB league does to the
position. Two managers — Man-Coverage Meerkats and End-Around Egrets — were still
waiting after the run tailed off, and in this format that is a spot you do not
want to be in.

Running back: 26 drafted, five of them in round 1, the first at 1.01. Gridiron
Gophers took the most with six. More than a third of every pick in the draft was
a running back, which tells you how this rookie class was valued in the room.

Tight end: ten drafted. The early window was 2.02 and 2.04 — two managers who
were not going to stream the position. Then it went quiet: the third tight end,
Mason Taylor, did not come off the board until 3.03, eleven picks after the
second one. After that it was a slow trickle to ten.

## The Picks We'll Be Arguing About in December

Blitz Alpacas' swing: Jalen Royals at 2.11 against Lan Larison at 6.11. One of
those is a fine pick and one of those might be the steal of the draft, and we
will not know which until the season tells us.

The board's biggest reach by Pull-Guard Pumas: Ashton Jeanty at 1.01, three
slots ahead of consensus. If he is a top-three rookie, no one remembers the
reach. If he is not, the Pumas hear about it every week.

Pull-Guard Pumas came away with the draft's top grade, an A+, on the strength of
twelve picks and seven of them funneled into round four. Whether that is a masterclass
in depth or a pile of round-four fliers is the argument, and both sides already
have their evidence.

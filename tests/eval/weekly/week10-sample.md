Trench Warfare — Week 10 Recap

## The Lead

Ten weeks in, and this season has finally stopped pretending its shape is a
mystery. The top of the table has separated itself, the middle has compressed
into a knot where one bad Sunday costs three places, and the bottom has begun
making peace with next year. The model and the standings agree on nearly
everything this week, which is either the sound of a league sorting itself out
or the quiet before a very loud surprise.

The week's loudest statement came from the league leader, who did not need a
close game to make a point. The quietest came from a contender that won ugly,
which is still winning, and is still the kind of result that decides a seed in
December.

## Around the League

- The league leader opened an early gap and never let it close, turning a
competitive matchup into a formality well before the afternoon slate was over.

- The second seed survived a scare, winning by the narrowest margin the week
produced and looking relieved rather than pleased about it.

- A middle-of-the-table side posted the week's biggest score and climbed, which
is what happens when a good week lands on a soft schedule.

- The week's most lopsided result went the way everyone expected, and the losing
side is running out of weeks to fix it.

- Two rivals played to a near-draw that neither will enjoy rewatching, and the
tiebreaker nobody understands is now doing real work.

- The bottom of the table produced the week's most entertaining game, which says
a good deal about the bottom of the table.

## Standings and the Playoff Picture

The playoff picture is starting to harden. The top group has opened enough of a
gap that a collapse, not a hot streak, is what it would take to miss. The middle
is the opposite problem: everybody there is one good Sunday from the bracket and
one bad Sunday from the consolation ladder.

The bubble is where the season lives now. Two sides are tied on record and
separated only by points, which is the sort of tiebreak that decides a January
and gets argued about for years. Nobody on the outside is mathematically out
yet, but the margin for error has shrunk to almost nothing.

## The Luck Index

The luck column stayed quiet, which is its own kind of story. The sides that
have been winning close games kept winning close games, and the sides that have
been losing them kept losing them.

That is not luck so much as habit. A team that wins every close one is doing
something right in the fourth quarter, and a team that loses every close one is
usually doing something wrong in the third.

## Next Week

Next week is the one that sorts the middle. Three of the four bubble sides play
each other, which means somebody is going to gain ground and somebody is going
to lose it, and the tiebreakers get simpler either way.

The top group gets a soft landing, which is exactly the week a contender should
be scared of. Nothing is easier to lose than a game that does not look
dangerous.

## The Transaction Desk

The wire stayed quiet again. No trades, no meaningful adds, just the usual churn
at the edges of rosters that are already out of the race.

That is not necessarily a mistake. In a league this deep, the managers who are
still working the wire are usually the ones who need it most, and the ones who
are standing pat are usually the ones who can afford to.
